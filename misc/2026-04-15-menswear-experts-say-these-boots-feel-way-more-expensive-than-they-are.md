What to Consider

Upper Material

Leather is always the safest bet, especially full-grain or nubuck, because it’s tough, ages well, and keeps your feet dry in wet conditions. Suede is softer and stylish, but it requires a little more TLC to keep it looking clean.

Midsole Material

EVA foam midsoles are lightweight and cushioned, perfect for long days on your feet. Some boots use polyurethane midsoles, which are a bit heavier but offer more durability and stability, especially if you’re on rugged terrain.

Outsole Material

Your traction and durability depend on this part. Vibram outsoles are often considered the gold standard for their grip and long-lasting wear. Rubber outsoles are great too. They’re flexible, waterproof, and give solid traction on the street or trail. Look for Goodyear welt stitching; it makes the boot more durable, more water-resistant, and fully resoleable.
