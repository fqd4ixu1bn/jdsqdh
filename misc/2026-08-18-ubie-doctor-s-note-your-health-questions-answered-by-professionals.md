Which medications should patients with sleep apnea avoid due to sedation risk?

People with sleep apnea should avoid medications that cause sedation or relax the muscles of the throat and airway, as these can worsen breathing interruptions during sleep. Key medications to avoid include: - **Benzodiazepines** (e.g., diazepam, lorazepam) — relax airway muscles and suppress breathing. - **Opioids** (e.g., oxycodone, morphine) — slow breathing and reduce oxygen levels. - **Sedative sleep aids** (e.g., zolpidem, eszopiclone) — can deepen airway collapse. - **Muscle relaxants** (e.g., cyclobenzaprine) — further loosen throat muscles. - **Certain antihistamines** (e.g., diphenhydramine) — cause drowsiness and airway relaxation. - **Alcohol and barbiturates** — depress the central nervous system and worsen apnea episodes. If you suspect you have sleep apnea or are unsure whether your current medications are safe, understanding your symptoms is the critical first step. Why am I having sudden blurred vision and dizziness when standing up?

Feeling dizzy with blurry vision when standing up is often caused by **orthostatic hypotension**, a sudden drop in blood pressure that temporarily reduces blood flow to the brain and eyes. When you stand quickly, gravity pulls blood toward your legs, and if your body can't compensate fast enough, symptoms like lightheadedness, blurred vision, or even fainting (orthostatic syncope) can occur. Common triggers include dehydration, prolonged bed rest, certain medications, and underlying conditions affecting circulation or the nervous system. Why can antibiotics cause yeast infections?
