Metformin and Vitamin B12 Deficiency: What to Know

Metformin, a widely prescribed medication for type 2 diabetes, can cause vitamin B12 deficiency, particularly with long-term or high-dose use. Studies suggest that up to 30% of people taking metformin may develop lower B12 levels over time. This happens because metformin can interfere with the absorption of B12 in the small intestine.

Symptoms of B12 deficiency include fatigue, weakness, numbness or tingling in the hands and feet, memory issues, and mood changes. Left unaddressed, it may lead to nerve damage or anemia. Regular B12 monitoring and supplementation may help those on metformin maintain healthy levels.

Explanation

How Does This Happen?

Vitamin B12 Absorption: Metformin can affect how the body absorbs vitamin B12 from food. Over time, this may lead to lower levels of this important vitamin.

Monitoring: People taking metformin, especially for a long time, should have their vitamin B12 levels checked regularly.
