What Is Propolis?

Propolis is a resin-like mixture bees create by collecting sap from trees and mixing it with beeswax and their own enzymes. This "bee glue" serves as a protective barrier in the hive, sealing cracks and defending against bacteria, fungi, and viruses. Its key components include:

Flavonoids (e.g., pinocembrin, galangin): Potent antioxidants and antimicrobials

Phenolic acids (e.g., caffeic acid phenethyl ester – CAPE): Anti-inflammatory and immune-modulating

Vitamins (B1, B2, B6, C, E) and minerals (magnesium, calcium, zinc)

How Propolis Is Used in Dental Practice

Dentists incorporate propolis into various formulations, often as an adjunct to standard treatments like scaling and root planing. Common delivery methods include:

Propolis-infused Mouthwash– Daily rinse to reduce bacterial load– Used pre-procedure to improve oral hygiene

Propolis Toothpaste or Gel– Twice-daily brushing to maintain gum barrier– Helps manage mild gingivitis at home

Topical Propolis Paste– Applied directly to problematic gum pockets– Supports healing after deep cleaning or minor surgery

Propolis-Coated Dental Devices– Slow-release strips or chips placed in periodontal pockets– Delivers sustained antimicrobial action

How Medical Doctors View Propolis for Oral Health

Physicians, especially those in integrative or functional medicine, appreciate propolis for its systemic benefits:

Overall Immune SupportPropolis's flavonoids and phenolics can bolster immune resilience, reducing susceptibility to infections that might worsen gum disease.

Antioxidant DefenseBy neutralizing free radicals systemically, propolis contributes to reduced oxidative stress, which is linked to periodontal disease progression.

Adjunct in Chronic ConditionsPatients with diabetes or autoimmune disorders may see more pronounced gum issues; propolis can be part of a broader immune-supportive plan.

Evidence from Clinical Studies

A randomized controlled trial in the Journal of Periodontology found that patients using 5% propolis mouthwash twice daily showed a significant reduction in plaque index and gingival inflammation compared to placebo.*

A meta-analysis in Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine concluded that propolis-based interventions consistently improved pocket depth and clinical attachment levels in periodontal therapy.**

In diabetic patients, propolis supplementation led to better glycemic control and slower progression of periodontitis, highlighting the link between systemic health and gum immunity.***
