What causes hair loss in the first place?

[Hair Loss](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/)

Hair loss can occur under many circumstances. There are obvious influences like genetics but also unexpected factors like medication side effects or hormonal disorders.

The most common form of hair loss is androgenetic alopecia (sometimes called androgenic alopecia).4 The primary driver behind androgenetic alopecia is the hormone dihydrotestosterone (DHT). When you produce too much DHT, it messes with your hair follicles, and those follicles shed their strains and have trouble creating new ones. In time, as this happens to more and more follicles, you experience hair loss.5

The most effective way researchers have found to combat DHT accumulation is to stop your body from creating so much of it to begin with. That requires a little dance with an enzyme called 5-alpha reductase. Your body typically uses 5-alpha reductase to turn testosterone into DHT, so doctors will prescribe 5-alpha reductase inhibitors to slow this conversion and allow your follicles to breathe again.

But medication can’t address every cause of hair loss. For example, telogen effluvium is a type of hair loss caused by physical or psychological stress on the body like chemotherapy, an accident, or a bad breakup.6 Your DHT levels have nothing to do with this kind of hair loss, so a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor won’t help.

Hair loss can also result from an autoimmune disorder called alopecia areata, in which your body’s immune system attacks the hair follicles.7 Alopecia areata can be localized, causing a patch of baldness, or it can go so far as to affect the whole body, rendering you completely hairless. None of the hair loss treatments you’ll find from Hims or its competitors can treat alopecia areata.

Do Hims hair loss products work?

Hims hair loss products should be effective for most men in the early-to-middle stages of hair loss, as they have the potential to stop further loss and revive dormant follicles.

In a placebo-controlled study looking at more than 1,500 men using finasteride 1mg for up to two years, men on finasteride saw a greater than 10% increase in hair count within target areas.8 Another study pitting topical finasteride against oral finasteride showed nearly identical results between the two.3

On the minoxidil side, studies of its topical application reveal increases in scalp coverage and patient satisfaction at the 5% concentration, which is the minimum employed by Hims.9 Oral minoxidil has produced similar, occasionally superior results to its topical counterpart, and it does so without the inconvenient regimen topical minoxidil involves.10

However, these medications can’t resurrect follicles from the dead if they’ve been starved of nutrients for too long. Also, you have to keep taking the product for as long as you want to keep your hair. Once you stop, your hair loss resumes.

Are Hims hair loss products safe?

While finasteride and minoxidil have been shown to be effective in treating hair loss, they aren’t without their risks. Here’s what you can expect from each:

Oral finasteride

In about 2-3% of patients, oral finasteride can cause unwanted sexual side effects, including reduced libido, infertility, and erectile dysfunction.11 Over the years covering men’s hair loss, we’ve spoken to many men who’ve experienced these effects, which can severely impact a person’s quality of life.

Finasteride is also capable of increasing symptoms of depression, which is why any good intake screener (including the one Hims employs) will ask you about your depression history when you seek hair loss treatment.12

Oral minoxidil

Oral minoxidil does not suffer from the same potential sexual side effects you see in finasteride, but that doesn’t mean it’s completely safe. As it was originally developed to treat high blood pressure, minoxidil may lower blood pressure in men taking it for hair loss.13 That said, hypertension doses range between 10mg and 40mg daily compared to the 1mg-5mg daily dose applied in hair loss cases. And some research suggests that minoxidil doesn’t exert a hypotensive effect in patients with normal blood pressure.14

Oral minoxidil has also been associated with a high rate of hypertrichosis, or the unwanted growth of hair on body parts other than the scalp.

Topical finasteride or minoxidil

Though topical finasteride and minoxidil get around the unpleasant side effects associated with their oral counterparts, they can cause itching, burning, scaling, or other discomfort at the application site.15
