What Are Heart Palpitations and Exhaustion?

Heart palpitations feel like your heart is racing, pounding, fluttering or skipping beats.

Exhaustion goes beyond ordinary tiredness—it's a deep, persistent fatigue that rest doesn't fix.

When these two issues occur together, you may notice:

A thumping or fluttering chest that wakes you up

Feeling drained or unable to fall back asleep

Dizziness, lightheadedness or weakness

Shortness of breath or a sense of tightness in your chest

While occasional palpitations and fatigue can be linked to stress, caffeine or lack of sleep, persistent nighttime episodes require careful attention.

Common, Less Serious Triggers

Before diving into red-flag scenarios, it helps to know everyday factors that can cause exhaustion and heart palpitations:

Excess caffeine, nicotine or energy drinks

Alcohol binges or withdrawal

High levels of stress or anxiety

Dehydration or electrolyte imbalance (low potassium, magnesium)

Overexertion—especially late-day workouts

Poor sleep hygiene (irregular bedtimes, screens in bed)

Often, reducing stimulants, rehydrating and improving sleep habits brings relief. But if palpitations and fatigue persist or worsen, it's time to look deeper.

When It Could Be More Serious

Several medical conditions can cause nighttime flutters and severe tiredness. Key possibilities include:

Arrhythmias

Atrial fibrillation (AFib): rapid, irregular heartbeat that may cause fatigue, weakness or chest discomfort

Premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) or supraventricular tachycardia (SVT): skipped or extra beats, often felt as flutters

Heart Disease

Heart failure: weakened heart muscle can't pump effectively, leading to fluid buildup, breathlessness and constant tiredness

Ischemic heart disease: reduced blood flow to the heart may trigger palpitations and profound fatigue

Thyroid Disorders

Hyperthyroidism: an overactive thyroid can speed up your heart and leave you jittery, sleepless and drained

Anemia

Low red blood cell count forces your heart to beat faster to deliver oxygen, causing palpitations and chronic exhaustion

Electrolyte Abnormalities

Imbalances in potassium, calcium or magnesium disrupt normal heart rhythms and energy production

Sleep Apnea

Repeated nighttime breathing pauses spike stress hormones, fragment sleep and overload your heart

Why Nighttime Matters

Reduced Distractions: When the house is quiet, you're more likely to notice every flutter.

Supine Position: Lying flat can change blood flow and make palpitations more pronounced.

Hormonal Fluctuations: Growth hormone, cortisol and other regulators cycle at night. Imbalances can trigger symptoms.

Delayed Stress Response: Your body may hold onto daytime stress, releasing it once you're in bed.

Because these factors converge at night, any underlying issue is amplified in the darkest hours—both literally and figuratively.

Warning Signs and Red Flags

While occasional palpitations paired with tiredness might not spell danger, watch for:

Chest Pain or Pressure: A squeezing sensation, burning or crushing pain

Fainting or Near-Fainting (Syncope): Losing consciousness or feeling you might pass out

Practical Steps You Can Take Now

While waiting to see a healthcare provider, you can:

Track your symptoms in a journal or app

Note time of day, duration, activities, food/drink intake and stress levels

Limit stimulants—cut back on caffeine, nicotine and energy drinks

Stay hydrated and maintain balanced electrolytes (eat bananas, leafy greens, nuts)

Practice calming bedtime routines: deep breathing, stretching, reading (no screens)

Keep a regular sleep schedule, even on weekends

Avoid heavy meals or alcohol close to bedtime

When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

Call emergency services (for example, dial 911) or go to the nearest emergency department if you experience:

Chest pain lasting more than a few minutes

Palpitations accompanied by fainting or near-fainting

Sudden, severe shortness of breath, especially at rest

Rapid, irregular heart rate above 150 bpm that won't slow

Confusion, significant weakness or inability to stand

Time can be muscle: quick action in a heart attack or dangerous arrhythmia can be life-saving.

Partnering with Your Doctor

At your appointment, expect your physician to:

Review your medical history and medications

Perform a physical exam, checking heart rate, blood pressure and lung sounds

Order an electrocardiogram (ECG/EKG) to spot arrhythmias

Check blood counts, thyroid function and electrolytes

Possibly recommend a Holter monitor (24- to 48-hour heart rhythm recorder)

Evaluate for sleep apnea with a sleep study if indicated

Open communication is vital. Bring your symptom log, share how episodes affect your daily life and ask questions until you fully understand your care plan.

Final Thoughts

Nighttime heart flutters and unshakeable exhaustion can be an early warning sign of something needing medical attention. While lifestyle tweaks often help, never ignore persistent or severe symptoms. If you're in doubt, it's always better to be seen and reassured—or treated—sooner rather than later.

Remember: if you have any life-threatening or serious concerns, speak to a doctor immediately. Your health and peace of mind are worth it.
