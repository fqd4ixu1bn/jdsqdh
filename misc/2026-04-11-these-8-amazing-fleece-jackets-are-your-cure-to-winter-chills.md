What to Consider

Material

Look for high-quality fleece materials that offer softness, durability, and warmth. Options like Polartec or high-pile fleece provide excellent insulation and comfort.

Weight

Fleece jackets come in various weights, from lightweight to heavy. Choose a weight based on how you plan to use it—lightweight for layering or active pursuits, and heavier for maximum warmth in colder conditions.

100 GSM

Light to moderate warmth, ideal for mild to moderately cool conditions, or for active use where freedom of movement and lighter weight are key.

150-200 GSM

Provides more warmth, suitable for colder weather or those who want extra insulation. This level is common in winter jackets that still need to balance warmth and comfort.

200+ GSM

Offers substantial warmth for very cold or extreme conditions, but it can be bulkier and heavier, which may limit mobility.

Style

Fleece jackets come in various styles, including full-zip, half-zip, pullover, and those with high collars or hoods. Choose a style that suits your personal taste and the occasions you’ll wear it for. Consider the pocket design and placement. Zippered pockets can provide security for your belongings, while hand pockets add convenience and warmth.

Abrasion Resistance

If you plan on using your fleece jacket for outdoor activities, look for features like abrasion-resistant overlays in high-wear areas to enhance durability.

Weather Resistance

Some fleece jackets come with water-repellent finishes or are designed to be worn under a shell jacket. If you expect to encounter rain or snow, consider these features.

What Type of Fleece is the Best?

Choosing the best type of fleece starts with determining your intended use of it. There’s various types to select from, including polar, microfleece, and textured fleece. Polar fleece is known for its heavyweight and fluffy feel as well as its insulation and superior warmth. The material is commonly often used in blankets, coats, jackets and sweaters. Microfleece is on the lighter side of the spectrum, offering a more breathable and thinner quality. Similar to polar fleece, textured fleece is thicker and features a patterned fabric. Fleece jackets also come in four different styles: hoodies, pullovers (sometimes with sap closures), half- and full-zip jackets.
