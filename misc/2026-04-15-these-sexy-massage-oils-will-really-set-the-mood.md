What to Look for in a Massage Oil

Ultimately, what you are looking for in a massage oil is going to depend on exactly how and where you are hoping to use the oil.

If you are looking for an oil that supports sports-recovery and muscle-repair, you’re going to want to opt for a product made with ingredients known to soothe sore muscles, such as arnica, CBD, and peppermint. Meanwhile, if you are looking for an oil that can double as a lubricant — or at the very least is safe for internal use — you’ll want an oil that is glycerin, fragrance, and paraben free.

So, before you overnight yourself one or three of the massage oils on this list, ask yourself:

What kind of massage am I hoping to receive or deliver with this massage oil?

Am I primarily going to be using this during partnered play or solo play?

Do I want this oil to double as a lubricant, or do I already have a lube I like?

Is it a problem if the product is not latex-compatible?

You also want to look at the ingredient list and make sure that there aren’t any ingredients that you’re allergic to. “Many massage oils use a nut-based carrier oil, so be on the lookout for that if you are allergic,” says Deysach. Indeed, almond oil and coconut oil are in the majority of massage oils on the market today.

Finally, consider your own scent preferences. It’s going to be hard to enjoy your massage if your nose is scrunched up in dis-pleasure. “While scented arousal oils can add to the experience by incorporating the sense of smell, if you don’t like the scent, it can detract from the experience,” she says. How Do You Remove Stains from Massage Oil?

The one (and there’s only one) downside of using massage oil is that if the oil drips onto your sheets, intimate wear, or other upholstery, it can stain. Sigh.

Thankfully, playing Susie or Sam Homemaker will do the trick. “Simply applying some stain remover or a baking soda paste to the area, then leaving it on for a bit before washing, is adequate for getting most oil stains out,” says Deysach. Shout, Dreft and oxygenated products, such as Oxi-Clean or Soil Love all work well, according to Stewart.

Another option is to have a dedicated set of sheets as your "for oil use only" sheets, she says. Or, to get a sex blanket (or two) which are often water-absorbent and make for easy-peasy clean-up after sex of all kinds.

“If you don’t want to risk stains from oil, you can always use massage lotions which won't stain your sheets and clothing,” says Stewart. Just be warned that while massage lotions offer a similar velvety feel, they typically absorb into the skin more quickly than massage oils, so you’ll have to apply them more often for the same consistently slick sensation.
