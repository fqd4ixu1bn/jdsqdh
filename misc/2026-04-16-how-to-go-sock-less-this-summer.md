# ​How to Go Sock-Less This Summer

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

Men are showing a lot of ankle this summer. Maybe because it’s one of the easiest ways to dress down a suit. Plus, the idea of pulling socks up your sweaty ankles is less than appealing as soon as the temperature goes above 80. So ditch the socks, roll up your cuffs, and feel the breeze. Only one problem: while your ankles are happy, your feet may not be.

[Sock-Less This](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/blog/2322599056.html)

That’s right, when you go sock-free, your feet are probably sweating in your sneakers or dress shoes. Suddenly they’re without the protective, absorbent barrier they once enjoyed. We know you’re not wearing sandals to work. And men’s shoes tend to be the lace-up, full-closure kind. So how are you supposed to let your ankles breathe while your feet suffocate?

With a few of the right moves, you’ll nail the look without sacrificing foot comfort. Just follow the five tips here.

Choose the right shoe.

We’re assuming you’re not wearing open-toed shoes. (If you are, socks aren’t even an option. Don’t be that guy.) But in the world of closed-toed shoes there’s a big difference between bucks and boots.

You’ll be happiest in a shoe with the lowest ankle line possible. That’s why loafers and slip-ons are so popular for this look. They’re summery, but also sensible—more of your foot gets to see the light of day, and the inside of the shoe isn’t as clammy.

Beyond shoe type, think about materials. A supple leather will feel better than a stiff one. Perforated leather will let your toes breathe. And a classic canvas sneaker is your lightweight option. (If you need any sneaker suggestions, read this.)

Give your shoes a day off.

You might actually be surprised by how good your Oxfords feel without socks. Chances are they’re some of the best-made shoes in your closet. We’ve seen men convert from “ew, how can you wear them like that” to “forget socks” in a single try.

But if you like it, don’t like it too much. At least, not with the same shoe. Sock-less looks mean your sole is intimate with your insole, sweating throughout the day. Give your shoes a 24-hour rest between wears so they can dry out.

Protect yourself against fungus.

[How to Go](https://dermaloom.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/3695649106.html)

Speaking of intimacy and sweat, your sock-less escapades may expose you to more foot fungus. Moisture created by your feet sweating is soaked up by your shoe. Also, whatever bacteria you stepped in barefoot in the locker room have now been transferred to the leather.

Good news: you can keep things in check with preventative moves. Make sure your feet are as dry as possible after a shower or workout, before putting on shoes. As an extra measure, you can sprinkle an anti-fungal powder on your feet, or directly in your shoes, just to keep worries at bay.

Take care of “hot spots.”

As your feet and shoes get to know each other, things may get heated. New areas of friction will appear—and friction leads to blisters.

With blisters, the key is to catch them before they become full-fledged, pus-filled nuisances. When you feel a hot spot—an area of friction where your foot is rubbing your shoe—protect it with a moleskin bandage. We like the pre-cut ones in this foot care kit (Coghlans Foot Care Kit, $8, amazon.com). The donut shape fits around the area in question, and prevents your shoe from rubbing against that spot.

And if it’s all too much for you: wear secret socks.

If you made it this far, you’re at least intrigued by the idea of going sock-free. (Just think, one less thing to wash and fold this summer.) But if you simply can’t stand it, you can still do the look. For you, there are no-show socks—and we have a full guide on them.

One caution: Just don’t forget you’re wearing them when you kick off your shoes in front of other people. As the name suggests, no-show socks aren’t supposed to be seen.
