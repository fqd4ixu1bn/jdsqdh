Explanation

Varicose veins—those bulging, twisted veins often seen on the legs—affect millions of people worldwide. While they're usually harmless, they can lead to discomfort, fatigue, and in some cases, more serious complications. Understanding which varicose veins treatment options are evidence-based and which you can safely skip helps you get relief without wasted time or money.

Why Varicose Veins Happen

Veins carry blood back toward the heart using one-way valves. If these valves weaken or fail, blood pools in the vein, causing it to stretch and twist. Risk factors include:

Family history

Age (over 50)

Female sex (hormones, pregnancy)

Obesity

Prolonged standing or sitting

History of blood clots

What Doctors Recommend

1. Lifestyle Changes

Doctors agree that simple daily habits can make a big difference:

Exercise Regularly• Walking, swimming or cycling improves circulation.• Aim for 30 minutes most days of the week.

Leg Elevation• Prop your legs up above heart level for 15–20 minutes, 2–3 times a day.

Weight Management• Even modest weight loss reduces pressure on veins.

Avoid Long Periods of Standing or Sitting• Move every 30 minutes—stand, stretch or take a short walk.

2. Compression Stockings

Compression stockings remain the first-line medical treatment for mild to moderate varicose veins. They:

Apply graduated pressure (strongest at ankles, lighter up thigh)

Improve blood flow back to the heart

Reduce swelling, aching and heaviness in the legs

Speak to a doctor or trained fitter about the right compression level (measured in mmHg).

3. Minimally Invasive Procedures

If lifestyle changes and stockings don't suffice, physicians often recommend one of these in-office therapies:

Endovenous Thermal Ablation• Uses laser or radiofrequency energy to close off the faulty vein.• Local anesthesia, quick recovery, high success rate.

Ultrasound-Guided Foam Sclerotherapy• A sclerosant foam is injected to scar and seal the vein.• Effective for smaller and medium veins.

Clarivein® (Mechanochemical Ablation)• Combines mechanical rotation with sclerosant infusion.• No heat, fewer bruises.

These methods typically allow you to walk out the same day and resume most activities within 24–48 hours.

4. Surgical Options

For extensive or complex varicose veins, surgery may be indicated:

Vein Ligation and Stripping• Tying off and removing the major saphenous vein.• Less common with modern ablation techniques but still used in certain cases.

Ambulatory Phlebectomy• Tiny incisions remove superficial veins.• Minimal scarring; local anesthesia.

Your vascular surgeon will help decide the best approach based on vein size, location and your overall health.

Additional Tips for Comfort

Wear loose-fitting clothes—tight waistbands or socks can impede blood flow.

Massage your calves gently in an upward direction—but avoid deep, aggressive massage over varicose veins.

Stay hydrated and maintain good skin care to prevent dryness and itching.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

While most varicose veins aren't life-threatening, watch for:

Sudden, severe leg pain or swelling (possible deep vein thrombosis)

Redness, warmth, and tenderness along a vein (phlebitis)

Non-healing skin ulcers near the ankle
