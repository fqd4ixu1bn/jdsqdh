What Is a Torn Achilles?

The Achilles tendon is one of the strongest tendons in our body, says Ryan Minara, D.P.M., assistant professor of orthopedics, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. Tendons connect muscle to bone, and the Achilles tendon connects our calf muscles to the bone of our heel. It's responsible for plantar flexing our foot, or moving the foot down.

"It's vital just for normal walking, push off power, but absolutely necessary for running, jumping and most athletic activity," Minara says. "When it ruptures, it really limits our ability to function normally, both in terms of regular walking, but definitely athletic activity."

How Long Does It Take To Recover From a Torn Achilles?

The answer depends on the person who has been injured. There are several ways to treat a torn Achilles, and the route of treatment typically depends on the age and activity level of the injured person.

Typically, Minara says, athletes will undergo surgical repair because there's only about 3 to 5 percent rate of re-rupture, as compared to a re-rupture rate of 25 percent with a nonsurgical repair. There are several different kinds of surgical routes. The severity of the tear will determine which kind of surgery is performed.

Generally, Minara says, a patient is on crutches for about 2 weeks post-operation. From 2 to 4 weeks post-op, patients will begin small range of motion exercises. From 4 to 6 weeks, patients will begin putting some weight through the tendon with the support of a boot of some kind. From weeks 6 to 8, they'll start to fully weight-bear with the boot. The boot will start to come off in about 8 to 12 weeks, and more intense physical therapy will begin to gain mobility and stability back to the tendon and ankle joint.

For an athlete, though, this timeline may speed up, as they have some high levels of strength that the common person may not have, Minara says. Age also contributes to the speediness of the healing process.

Achilles tears do "have the potential to be a kind of life-changing devastating injury; it's very hard to get back to 100 percent," Minara says. That doesn't mean it's not possible for Rodgers, but "his age is not working in his favor. The real question becomes whether or not if he can rehab enough to play again, starting back next season."

The Jets managed to still pull out a first game win against the Bills in overtime with a final score of 22 to 16, led by Jets back-up quarterback Zach Wilson. The team posted an Instagram earlier today, wishing Rodgers a speedy recovery.

Rodgers has yet to comment on the injury.
