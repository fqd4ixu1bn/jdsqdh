What are the early signs of brain-eating amoeba?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Early signs of a brain-eating amoeba (Naegleria fowleri) infection typically include:

Severe headache (often frontal)

Fever

Nausea and vomiting

Stiff neck

These symptoms usually appear 1 to 9 days after exposure, most commonly within 5 days of contact with warm freshwater. As the infection progresses, it can rapidly cause confusion, seizures, hallucinations, and loss of balance. Because early symptoms closely mimic bacterial meningitis and the illness advances quickly, prompt recognition is critical.

Explanation

The brain-eating amoeba, also known as Naegleria fowleri, can cause an infection called primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM). This is a rare but severe disease that affects the brain. Early signs of this infection may include:

[Symptoms Ubie Doctor's Note](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/8880327068.html)

Headache: This is often one of the first symptoms.

Fever: A high temperature usually follows the headache.

Nausea and Vomiting: These can occur as the infection progresses.

These symptoms can appear anywhere from one to nine days after the amoeba enters the body, typically through the nose during swimming or other water activities. As the disease progresses, more severe symptoms such as confusion, seizures, and hallucinations can develop. If you're experiencing any concerning symptoms after exposure to warm freshwater, you can check your symptoms with our free AI symptom checker and seek medical attention immediately, as early detection is critical.
