Important Bone Info: Why Long Fasts Harm Skeletal Strength

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Extended juice cleanses and prolonged fasts can harm bone health by depriving the body of essential nutrients like calcium, vitamin D, and protein. This nutrient deficit triggers hormonal changes that accelerate bone breakdown and causes muscle loss, which reduces the mechanical loading bones need to stay strong—ultimately increasing bone density loss and fracture risk.

To protect your bones, focus on a balanced diet of whole foods, weight-bearing exercise, targeted supplementation, limited fasting duration, and guidance from a healthcare professional.

Because bone loss often develops silently, identifying warning signs early is critical. Explanation

Juice cleanses and other prolonged fasting protocols have gained popularity for rapid weight loss, detox promises, and ease of use. However, extended periods of low‐calorie intake can carry hidden risks—especially for your bones. Bone is living tissue that requires a steady supply of nutrients, hormones, and mechanical stress to stay strong. Here's how long fasts like juice cleanses can compromise skeletal health, and what you can do to protect your bones.

1. Nutrient Deficiencies Undermine Bone Building

Bones depend on a consistent intake of specific nutrients. During a juice cleanse or extended fast, you may fall short on:

Calcium• Essential for bone mineralization.• Adults need roughly 1,000–1,300 mg/day (NIH).

Vitamin D• Aids calcium absorption in the gut.• Deficiency slows new bone formation.

Protein• Supplies amino acids for collagen in bone matrix.• Low protein intake tilts bone remodeling toward loss.

Other micronutrients• Magnesium, vitamin K, zinc and phosphorus all play roles in bone strength.

Without these building blocks, bone formation slows while resorption (breakdown) continues, leading to net loss of bone density.

2. Hormonal Shifts Increase Bone Resorption

Long fasts trigger hormonal changes that can weaken bones:

Lowered IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1)• Fasting reduces IGF-1, a key growth factor that stimulates bone-forming cells (osteoblasts).

Decreased sex hormones• Women: estrogen levels drop when energy intake is too low; estrogen protects against bone loss.• Men: testosterone can decline, also reducing bone formation.

4. Muscle Loss Reduces Mechanical Loading

Mechanical stress from muscles pulling on bones stimulates bone formation (Wolff's law). During long fasts:

Lean muscle mass decreases when calories—and especially protein—are insufficient.

Reduced muscle mass means less force on bone, so bones receive fewer "growth" signals.

Weak muscles also increase fall risk, compounding fracture potential.

Maintaining muscle through adequate protein and resistance exercise is vital for bone strength.

5. Increased Risk of Fractures and Long-Term Bone Density Loss

The combined effects above translate into real‐world risks:

Short term:• Joint pain and muscle cramps (early warning signs).• Heightened susceptibility to stress fractures (especially in runners, dancers).

Long term:• Accelerated bone density loss.• Early onset osteopenia or osteoporosis.• Increased lifetime fracture risk, especially hip and spine.

Juice Cleanse vs. Balanced Nutritional Strategy

Juice cleanses often last 3–7 days or more. During that time, calorie intake may drop well below what your bones need. A more balanced approach might include:

Whole foods: fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, whole grains.

Calcium-rich options: low-fat dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens.

Vitamin D sources: fatty fish, egg yolks, sensible sun exposure.

Protein distribution: 20–30 g per meal to support muscle—and bone—health.

Weight-bearing exercise: walking, jogging, resistance training.

If you're considering a juice cleanse, keep these adjust­ments in mind to minimize bone density loss.
