# Former Olympic Runner Nick Symmonds Looks Ripped After Putting on 21 Pounds of Muscle

When you think of Olympic sprinters, you probably don’t picture them packing on muscle. Coming off the blocks and tearing down the track is usually associated with a lithe, compact frame; it’s an over-simplification, sure, but it’s intuitive to figure that a sprinter’s body isn’t going to look like Arnold Schwarzenegger.

Former two-time Olympian Nick Symmonds, of course, knows that, but in a recent video he reveals that over the past few years he’s started lifting weights, as part of his quest to become a more well-rounded athlete. That’s a bit of a change: as an active Olympian, he says he’d lift two days a week, purely functional lifting. Now that he’s retired, though, he’s hitting the gym five days a week and, as he puts it, “lifting heavy.”

[Nick Symmonds Looks](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/4752382138.html)

Inevitably, that means he put on muscle. “I now weigh 21 pounds more than I did when I ran in the Olympic final,” he says, “but in some ways I feel healthier.” Running ten miles a day meant running off much of his natural body weight, he explains; today he’s more explosive, he says, even though he may be slower for carrying the extra weight—he bets today he could run a faster forty than back in his pro-running days.

Back then, his target race weight was 162 pounds. These days he’s weighing in at 183.6. How does that translate on the track? As he details in the video, his previous personal record for a 400 meters was 47.4 seconds.

With the extra muscle, he’s hitting 57.21 on the 400—a full 10 seconds slower. But as he explains, because of his strength-to-weight ratio, it’s actually the best 400 he’s run. “I really feel that today I am in the best shape of my life,” he says. “Overall, I am a much healthier athlete today than I was as a professional runner.”
