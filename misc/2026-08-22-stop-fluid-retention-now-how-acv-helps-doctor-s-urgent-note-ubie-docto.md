What Is Fluid Retention?

Fluid retention happens when tiny blood vessels (capillaries) leak fluid into nearby tissues and the lymphatic system can't clear it. Common sites include the ankles, feet, legs, hands, and abdomen.

Common Causes

High sodium intake

Hormonal changes (e.g., pre-menstrual syndrome)

Prolonged sitting or standing

Certain medications (steroids, blood pressure meds)

Underlying conditions (heart, liver, or kidney disease)

Signs and Symptoms

Puffiness or swelling in hands, legs, feet, ankles, or face

Stretched or shiny skin

Indentation ("pitting") when you press swollen area

Stiff joints or decreased mobility

What Research Says

While direct studies on ACV for edema are limited, several mechanisms support its use:

Diuretic Effects

Acetic acid may act as a mild diuretic, helping kidneys excrete extra water and sodium.

Animal studies suggest vinegar increases urine volume, which can translate to reducing fluid buildup.

Electrolyte Balance

Potassium in ACV can counterbalance excess sodium.

Maintaining optimal sodium-to-potassium ratio is key for preventing water retention.

Improved Circulation

Polyphenols in vinegar support blood vessel health, potentially reducing capillary leakage.

Better circulation helps lymphatic drainage, clearing excess fluid.

Alkalizing Effect

Though acidic in taste, ACV may have an alkalizing effect in the body's metabolism.

A balanced pH can support kidney function and fluid homeostasis.

When to See Your Doctor

While mild fluid retention may respond to diet, exercise, and natural remedies like ACV, certain warning signs require prompt medical attention:

Rapid or severe swelling

Shortness of breath, chest pain, or irregular heartbeat

Painful, red, or hot areas in swollen limbs (risk of deep vein thrombosis)

Sudden weight gain (more than 2–3 lbs in 24 hours)

Persistent swelling despite home measures

Important: If any of the above occur, seek medical help immediately or call emergency services.
