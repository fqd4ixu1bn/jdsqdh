Castor Oil for Women 65+: Natural Relief for Joint Pain & Aging Skin

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

For women 65+, castor oil may provide gentle, topical relief for joint discomfort and dry, aging skin. Its moisturizing and anti-inflammatory properties can soothe rough patches, and small studies suggest castor oil packs or massage may ease short-term osteoarthritis symptoms. However, castor oil does not reverse arthritis or repair damaged cartilage.

Key considerations include safe topical use, who should avoid it (such as pregnant women or those with certain skin conditions), why ingestion is not recommended, and red flags—like severe swelling, fever, or sudden joint deformity—that require prompt medical care.

Because joint pain in older women can stem from many causes—osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, bursitis, or even gout—self-treating without clarity may delay proper care. Castor Oil and Joint Pain in Older Women

Why Joint Pain Increases After 65

Joint discomfort becomes more common with age, often due to:

Wear and tear on cartilage

Reduced joint lubrication

Low‑grade inflammation

Conditions such as osteoarthritis (OA)

OA is especially common in women after menopause. If joint stiffness, swelling, or reduced mobility sounds familiar, taking a few minutes to complete a free Osteoarthritis (OA) symptom assessment can help you better understand your symptoms and prepare for a more informed conversation with your doctor.

How Castor Oil May Help Joints

Credible medical sources note that topical castor oil may support joint comfort by:

Reducing mild inflammation when applied to the skin

Improving circulation in the area during massage

Softening connective tissues, which may ease stiffness

Small clinical studies involving topical castor oil packs have shown short‑term pain reduction in people with knee discomfort, including those with osteoarthritis. These studies are limited in size, but results are encouraging enough that some clinicians consider castor oil a reasonable complementary option.

Important to know:Castor oil does not reverse arthritis or repair cartilage. Its role is supportive—not curative.

Using Castor Oil for Joint Comfort

Many women apply castor oil externally to joints such as knees, hands, hips, or shoulders.

A simple method:

Apply a small amount of castor oil to clean skin

Gently massage into the joint for 5–10 minutes

Cover with a soft cloth if desired

Use 3–4 times per week

Some women prefer "castor oil packs," which involve applying oil, covering with cloth, and adding gentle warmth. If you use heat, keep it mild and avoid sleeping with heating pads.

Safety Considerations for Women 65+

Castor oil is natural—but natural does not mean risk‑free.

Use with Caution If You:

Have very sensitive or broken skin

Are prone to allergic reactions

Have diabetes with reduced skin sensation

Have open wounds or infections

Do not ingest castor oil unless specifically directed by a doctor

When to Speak to a Doctor

Always speak to a doctor if you experience:

Persistent or worsening joint pain

Joint swelling, warmth, or redness

Pain that limits walking or daily activities

Sudden skin changes, sores, or infections

Numbness or loss of sensation
