What causes seizures to occur when falling asleep?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Seizures that happen while falling asleep are often linked to shifts in brain activity during the transition from wakefulness to sleep. This period involves changes in electrical signaling that can trigger seizures in people with sleep-related epilepsy syndromes, such as sleep-related hypermotor epilepsy (SHE) or juvenile myoclonic epilepsy. Common symptoms include sudden jerking movements, unusual vocalizations, or brief loss of awareness right as you drift off.

Because sleep-onset seizures can be mistaken for normal sleep twitches or other conditions, identifying the underlying cause matters for getting the right care.
