# Watch Strongman Eddie Hall Shoot for an Olympic-Worthy Shot Put

Strongman Eddie Hall has been mixing up his typical heavyweight training routines as he prepares for his much-anticipated boxing match against rival Hafthor Björnsson. Hall's showed off everything from his leg day workouts to his rest day cardio routine recently—but he's also kept it fun, playing frisbee with plates and arm wrestling. Now, he's having another fun training day and testing his power by trying out the Olympic shot put.

Hall goes about the attempt without any formal training, but that doesn't stop him. He decides to make it a competition with a friend, using a shot that weighs 7.26 kilos, which equals about 16 pounds. They also decide to do a quick Google search on the current shot put world record, which is 23.1 meters set by American Randy Barnes in 1990.

"Wow, it's stood for 30 years," says Hall. He looks up the record for an under-20 male, which is 18.7 meters, and decides to use that as his bench mark.

They pull out an open reel measuring tape to denote the measurement.

"I'm genuinely interested to see what I can do, because I mean it's great being big and strong, but transferring that into functional stuff like this it's a completely different concept," says Hall.

He wraps his wrist and chalks up his hands in preparation.

Pat, his friend, is up first. He puts the shot 9.3 meters.

"That's incredible," says Hall. "That's what we've got to do then."

Hall gives it a go with two poorly-executed spins, and gets to a length of 11.5 meters.

"I feel like I'm getting this now. The power's coming," says Hall.

He throws again, and gets to 13 meters—still more than 5 meters off the record he's looking to match, and nowhere near the level of control needed for a competitive throw.

His fourth throw reaches 13 meters, so he resets his goal to get to at least 15 meters.

On his fifth throw, he really gives it his all—and still only throws to 14.5 meters.

"I can't go anymore because I'm scared about ripping my shoulder out," says Hall. "For someone that has zero Olympic throwing experience, is that good? I do not know."
