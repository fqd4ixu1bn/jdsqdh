Why Does Wegovy Cause Fatigue?

Fatigue with Wegovy can stem from multiple, interrelated factors:

Gastrointestinal Changes

Slower digestion: With a slower emptying stomach, you may feel fuller but also less energetic, especially if you're eating smaller, less calorie-dense meals.

Nausea or discomfort: Common early side effects like nausea can make eating less appealing, reducing overall calorie and nutrient intake.

Reduced Calorie Intake

Calorie deficit: By design, Wegovy helps you eat less. A sudden drop in calories—especially if it's too steep—can lead to tiredness as your body adjusts to burning stored fat for energy.

Micronutrient gaps: Eating less without focusing on nutrient-dense foods can cause deficiencies (iron, B12, magnesium), which are well-known contributors to fatigue.

Fluid and Electrolyte Imbalance

Mild dehydration: Nausea or reduced drinking due to feeling full can leave you short on fluids, causing lightheadedness and lethargy.

Electrolyte shifts: Less food and water intake may disturb sodium, potassium, and other electrolyte levels, making you feel weak.

Blood Sugar Fluctuations

Hypoglycemia risk: Though rare in people without diabetes, lower blood sugar from decreased food intake can cause shakiness, sweating, and fatigue.

Energy swings: If you're accustomed to frequent meals or high-carb snacks, the transition to smaller meals may lead to dips in energy between meals.

Central Nervous System Effects

Direct GLP-1 action on the brain: GLP-1 receptors are found in energy-regulating centers of the brain. While the exact mechanisms aren't fully understood, altering these signals can sometimes lead to feelings of tiredness or mental fog.

Adjustment Period

Adaptation window: Most side effects—including fatigue—are most pronounced during dose escalation. Wegovy dosing starts low (0.25 mg weekly) and increases every four weeks to the maintenance dose (2.4 mg weekly).

Timeline: Fatigue often peaks in the first 4–8 weeks and then gradually improves as your body adapts.

Managing Fatigue on Wegovy

If you're experiencing tiredness, consider these practical strategies:

• Gradual Titration – Stick to the prescribed ramp-up schedule. Don't rush dose increases in hopes of faster weight loss. – Talk to your provider about a slower titration if fatigue is severe.

• Balanced Nutrition – Focus on nutrient-rich foods: lean proteins, whole grains, fruits, vegetables, nuts, and seeds. – Watch for signs of deficiency (e.g., hair loss, brittle nails, pale skin) and discuss testing for iron, B12, vitamin D, and magnesium.

• Hydration and Electrolytes – Aim for at least 8 cups of fluids daily, adjusted for activity level and climate. – Consider electrolyte-enhanced beverages or adding a pinch of salt to your water if approved by your doctor.

• Regular, Gentle Exercise – Light activities like walking, yoga, or stretching can boost energy levels. – Avoid overtraining; listen to your body and rest when needed.

• Sleep Hygiene – Prioritize 7–9 hours of sleep each night. – Establish a consistent bedtime routine and limit screens before sleep.

When to Consider Switching Medications

Wegovy may not suit everyone. You might think about a switch if:

• Persistent, Disabling Fatigue – Fatigue that doesn't improve after 8–12 weeks of dose stabilization – Impaired daily functioning (work, family, self-care)

• Significant Nutrient Deficiencies – Ongoing anemia or vitamin deficiencies despite supplementation – Unintended weight loss below target range

• Severe Gastrointestinal Side Effects – Recurrent nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain impacting quality of life

• Interactions with Other Medical Conditions – Pancreatitis history, gallbladder disease, or severe kidney problems

Discuss alternative options with your healthcare provider, which may include:

Other GLP-1 agonists with different side-effect profiles

Non-GLP-1 medications (e.g., bupropion/naltrexone, orlistat)

Comprehensive lifestyle and behavioral interventions
