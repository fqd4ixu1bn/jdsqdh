The digestive system explained

The digestion process involves various organs that make up the digestive system.

It starts in your mouth, where saliva acts on the food after chewing. The esophagus muscles then push the chewed food down to your stomach.

Stomach acids further break down the food before it makes its way to the small intestine. Digestive juices from other organs further work on the food, facilitating nutrient absorption. What’s left goes through the large intestine for water absorption.

The rest is waste that goes to the rectum and is excreted through the anus. Poor digestion can occur anywhere along the digestive system.

What is indigestion?

Indigestion is when you experience pain and discomfort after eating. Health practitioners use the term dyspepsia, which translates to indigestion.

Nausea or a stomach ache following a meal indicates an issue with your digestive system. It could be a reaction to the food or poor digestion of your meal.

Dyspepsia usually occurs for several minutes to several hours following a meal. It takes 3–5 hours for complete digestion of food from the stomach into the small intestines. Your pancreas and liver send digestive juices to help with digestion.

Indigestion may mean there’s an underlying gastrointestinal issue that requires medical attention.

Why does indigestion happen?

Eating habits contribute mainly to indigestion. Abdominal contractions produce potent digestive juices that irritate your stomach lining.

Indigestion also results from the stomach’s gastric acid interacting with mucosa. Mucosa is the delicate and protective lining of the digestive system. Painful inflammation and discomfort may result from the deterioration of the gastric mucosa.

In addition to food, many other things can induce stomach problems. They include smoking, alcohol consumption, pregnancy, anxiety, and certain medications.

Indigestion manifests in various forms. The primary symptom is invariably abdominal pain or discomfort. The discomfort is normally felt in your abdomen where most digestive organs are.

Indigestion usually manifests after a meal while the stomach is still digesting. The medical term for this is the postprandial phase.

Signs of an unhealthy gut

Abdominal pain, diarrhea, constipation, and bloating are signs of poor digestive health. There are more reasons that point to an unhealthy gut.

Let’s look at other poor digestion symptoms.

Stomach discomfort

Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) is a condition that affects the large intestines. It leads to constipation, gas, bloating, diarrhea, and abdominal discomfort.

Research shows dysbiosis may contribute to developing IBS. Dysbiosis is a condition where there’s a bacterial imbalance in the intestine. A healthy gut processes food correctly, leading to no stomach discomfort.

Unintentional weight gain or loss

Have you experienced weight fluctuation without modifying your diet or exercise regimen? It may indicate your gut is unhealthy.

An imbalance in the gastrointestinal tract affects your weight. It impedes the ability to store fat, assimilate nutrients, and regulate blood sugar.

Sugar cravings

It should come as no surprise that sugar is highly addictive. The presence of bacteria in the intestines may increase your desire for sugar.

A review showed your gut microbes can heighten cravings. The most effective method for banishing cravings is to reduce the intake of added sugars.

Sleep irregularity and constant fatigue

Do you get enough sleep but feel fatigued during the day? An unhealthy gut contributes to feelings of physical and mental fatigue. Constant lethargy is generally a result of poor mental health, metabolism, and inflammation.

A study established a correlation between fatigue and a gastrointestinal microbiome. Additionally, it showed gastrointestinal issues, like constipation, can cause fatigue.

Skin irritation and issues

Your gut bacteria working on refined sugar and fat may lead to skin breakouts. Studies show gut issues affect facial conditions like acne, psoriasis, and eczema.

Gut microbiota influences your skin through the immune system. Taking probiotics helps maintain a healthy gut and prevents skin irritation and inflammation.

Watch out for fats in your diet

Fatty foods are difficult to metabolize, leading to abdominal discomfort and heartburn. For improved abdominal comfort, reduce your consumption of high-fat foods.

Alternatively, go for skim or semi-skimmed milk, grilled dishes, lean meats, and fish. Omega-3 fatty acids reduce the risk of developing inflammatory gastrointestinal diseases. Salmon, chia seeds, flaxseeds, and sardines are all excellent sources of omega-3 fats.

Adequate fat consumption is necessary for proper digestion. The body requires fats to assimilate essential vitamins A, D, E, and K.

Anxiety and stress can impact each component of the digestive system. A network of nerves in the brain regulates a part of the gastrointestinal tract. Each intestinal lining possesses its neuronal network.

The sympathetic nervous system detects potentially stressful situations. It initiates the fight-or-flight response, which prepares the body to confront the threat.
