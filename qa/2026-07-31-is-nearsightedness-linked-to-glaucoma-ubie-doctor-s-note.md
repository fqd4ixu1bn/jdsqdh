# Is nearsightedness linked to Glaucoma? | Ubie Doctor's Note

Is nearsightedness linked to Glaucoma?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Nearsightedness (myopia) increases your risk of glaucoma, particularly primary open-angle glaucoma. Risk rises with severity: about 1.7x for low myopia, 2.5x for moderate, and 3.3x for high myopia.

Why myopia raises glaucoma risk: The elongated shape of a myopic eye stretches and thins the optic nerve and retinal tissue, making them more vulnerable to pressure damage. This structural change can also mask early signs of glaucoma on standard exams, delaying diagnosis.

Warning signs to watch for: gradual peripheral vision loss, blurred vision, halos around lights, and eye pain or pressure.

Recommended next steps: Adults with myopia should have comprehensive dilated eye exams every 1–2 years, including optic nerve imaging (OCT) and visual field testing. Treatment options include prescription eye drops, laser therapy, and surgery when needed.

Because myopic eyes can hide glaucoma until vision loss occurs, understanding your symptoms early is critical. Why Does Myopia Increase Glaucoma Risk?

A longer eye globe tugs on nerve fibers, reducing their resilience to pressure changes.

Myopic eyes may have compromised microcirculation around the optic nerve head.

Thinner RNFL in myopes can mask early glaucoma changes, delaying detection.

Recognizing Warning Signs

Glaucoma often progresses without symptoms until considerable vision is lost. However, you should pay attention to:

Painless peripheral vision loss ("tunnel vision")

Frequent changes in eyeglass prescription

Seeing halos around lights

Sudden eye pain, headache, nausea, and blurred vision (in acute angle-closure glaucoma)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does wearing glasses worsen my risk?A: No. Corrective lenses do not increase glaucoma risk; they simply help you see clearly.

Q: Can children with myopia get glaucoma?A: While rare, juvenile open-angle glaucoma can occur. Early eye exams are crucial for myopic kids.

Q: Is high myopia reversible?A: Orthokeratology and refractive surgery (LASIK) correct vision but do not change axial length or glaucoma risk.
