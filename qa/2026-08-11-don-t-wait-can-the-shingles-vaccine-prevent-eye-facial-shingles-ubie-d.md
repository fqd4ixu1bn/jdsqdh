Can the shingles vaccine help prevent facial or eye shingles?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

The shingles vaccine (Shingrix) can help prevent shingles on the face and eyes. It is over 90% effective at preventing shingles and postherpetic neuralgia in healthy adults aged 50 and older, including cases involving the ophthalmic nerve, which can affect the forehead, eyelids, and eyes. Facial or ocular shingles can lead to serious complications such as vision loss, so vaccination is strongly recommended for those eligible.

Explanation

The shingles vaccine is designed to prevent shingles, which is caused by the reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus. This virus can affect different parts of the body, including the face and eyes, a condition known as herpes zoster ophthalmicus.

How the Vaccine Helps: According to the sources, the shingles vaccine can significantly reduce the risk of developing shingles, including on the face or eyes. It works by boosting the immune system's ability to fight the virus.

Importance: Preventing shingles is important because when it affects the eyes, it can lead to serious complications such as vision problems. If you're experiencing unusual symptoms or want to understand your risk factors better, try our free AI symptom checker for personalized health insights.

Effectiveness: The vaccine is effective in reducing both the incidence and severity of shingles, making it a valuable tool in preventing herpes zoster ophthalmicus.

In summary, getting vaccinated can help protect against the painful and potentially serious effects of shingles affecting the face and eyes.
