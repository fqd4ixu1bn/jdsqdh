What sensations might one experience when waking up from a seizure?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Waking up after a seizure often causes confusion, fatigue, and headaches. Many people also experience nausea, muscle soreness, memory loss, or brief visual disturbances like seeing things that aren't there. This recovery phase, called the postictal state, can last minutes to hours and varies from person to person.

Because seizure symptoms overlap with many other conditions—including migraines, low blood sugar, fainting, and sleep disorders—it's important to identify the true cause quickly. Understanding what's behind your symptoms is the first step toward getting the right care. Explanation

Waking up from a seizure can be a confusing and tiring experience. People often feel disoriented or unsure about what just happened. This confusion can last for a short time or a bit longer.

Common Sensations:

Confusion: This is a common feeling. It may be hard to remember where you are or what you were doing before the seizure.

Tiredness: Seizures can be exhausting, and many people feel very tired afterward.

[Up From Seizure](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/2393743967.html)

Headache: Some people may have a headache when they wake up.

Other Possible Sensations:

Nausea: Sometimes, people may feel sick to their stomachs or even vomit.

Seeing Things: It's possible to see things that aren't really there, like lights or shapes. These are called hallucinations.

If you or someone you know is experiencing these sensations after a seizure, it may be helpful to check your symptoms using a free AI-powered symptom checker to better understand what you're experiencing and determine if you should seek medical attention.
