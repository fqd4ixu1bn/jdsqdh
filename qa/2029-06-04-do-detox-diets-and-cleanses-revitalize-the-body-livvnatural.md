Understanding Detox Diets and Cleanses

Thanks to their naturopathic origins, detox diets and cleanses are growing rapidly. What we eat or drink, pollution, and other contaminants can wreak havoc, causing serious ailments.

To date, the term “detoxing” has been used to refer to treatment of serious disorders like kidney failure or alcohol poisoning. More recently, it’s become known as the process of removing toxins from the body.

Cleanses and detox diets are believed to be more gentle on the digestive system and help with weight loss. They’re also believed to reduce the risk of serious illnesses.

These practices come in the form of liquid diets, a period of fasting, or restricting certain foods like sugar, dairy, or gluten. Many choose to supplement their diets with juices and other drinks to boost their vitamin intake.

As with anything in life, there are pros and cons to consider, as well as alternative perspectives.

The Controversies Around Detox Diets

Due to the lack of scientific evidence, few healthcare professionals and nutritional experts promote detox diets or cleanses. In fact, they’re more likely to highlight the potential risks that come with these practices.

What are the risks? Below, we’ve listed some of the potential side effects to look out for.

Practices that claim to clean the colon present the risk of bloating, excessive cramping, and nausea.

Prolonged detoxing or cleansing may cause dehydration with the depletion of electrolytes.

Weight loss may be quite rapid during detox diets or cleanses, but so is weight gain once you stop the protocol.

Improper calorie restrictions can result in fatigue, mineral deficiencies, and irritability, among other side effects.

With various perspectives to consider, it’s crucial to do your research and seek advice from professionals.

Professional Guidance and Individualized Plans

Cleanses and detox diets are straightforward enough to follow and many have had significant results. It’s always best, however, to consult a doctor before you start a regimen.

These diets often severely limit your nutrient and calorie intake and can pose various health risks. In some cases, a detox diet could be fatal. Below, we’ve listed the groups of at-risk populations.

Pregnant or lactating women

Children or adolescents

Older adults

Existing health problems, like malnourishment, eating disorder, or diabetes

If you fit in any of these groups, please have an open discussion with your healthcare practitioner before starting any detox or cleansing regimen. If they deem the approach safe to proceed, they’ll help you in determining the best course of action for your circumstances and goals.
