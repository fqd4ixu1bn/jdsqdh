Swollen tonsils are enlarged, inflamed tissues at the back of the throat, often caused by viral or bacterial infections (like strep throat), allergies, or chronic conditions such as tonsillitis. Symptoms may include sore throat, difficulty swallowing, fever, and visible redness or white patches. Treatment depends on the underlying cause and can range from rest and hydration to antibiotics, or in recurring cases, a tonsillectomy.

Explanation

Swollen tonsils, also known as tonsillitis, can occur due to infections like bacteria or viruses, or due to chronic conditions affecting the throat. Unilateral tonsillar enlargement can sometimes be a sign of more serious conditions and should be evaluated by a healthcare provider. Chronic tonsillitis can be linked to other issues like periodontal diseases, which affect the gums and teeth.

Treatment Options:

Antibiotics: For bacterial infections, antibiotics like clindamycin may be used to treat recurrent acute tonsillitis.

Medical Evaluation: Persistent or unusual enlargement should be assessed by a healthcare professional to rule out other causes.

Chronic Management: Addressing underlying chronic conditions may involve different strategies, including dental care if periodontal disease is present.
