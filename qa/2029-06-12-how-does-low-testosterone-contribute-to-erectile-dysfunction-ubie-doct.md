How does low testosterone contribute to erectile dysfunction?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Low testosterone can cause erectile dysfunction (ED) by lowering libido and weakening the nerve and blood flow signals needed to achieve and maintain an erection. Testosterone plays a key role in sexual desire and the physiological response that produces firmness, so when levels drop, both arousal and erection quality can suffer.

Because ED can stem from hormonal, vascular, psychological, or neurological causes, identifying the root issue is essential to finding the right treatment. Explanation

Testosterone is a hormone that helps the body function well, including supporting sexual desire and the ability to have an erection. When testosterone levels are low, the body might not send strong enough signals to get the blood flowing into the penis, which is needed for an erection. This means that a man's interest in sex can decrease, and even when he is attracted and ready, the physical process of achieving an erection might not work as well.

Research shows that low testosterone is linked to weaker erectile function because the hormone also helps keep the blood vessels and nerves in good working order. When testosterone drops, these systems may not work as effectively, making it harder to erect and maintain an erection during sexual activity. While many factors can cause erectile dysfunction, low testosterone is an important one.

If you or someone you know is having these issues, you can start by taking a free symptom checker assessment to better understand what might be causing your symptoms, and then speak with a doctor who can check hormone levels and help determine the best treatment to improve overall sexual function and quality of life.
